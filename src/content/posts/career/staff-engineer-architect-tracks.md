At senior, the next step is often **Staff** (or Principal) on an IC track. Some companies also have **Architect** as a parallel title. The healthy split: Staff is **scope of influence plus delivery** — a sticky problem, a team of teams, still close to code and incidents. Architect is **cross-cutting design authority** — standards, target landscapes, review boards — still useless if they never see production. Unhealthy: Architect as a drawing-only role, Staff as "senior but we ran out of manager heads."

## Archetypes (Larson)

Tech lead, architect (as a **role**, not necessarily the HR title), solver, right-hand. You can be Staff as a solver who exits a burning platform. You can be Architect as a staff-plus who owns the event taxonomy. The title should describe **how you are measured**: shipped outcomes and health of the systems you touch, not slide count.

Architect tracks fail when they cannot say no to a snowflake, or when they say no to everything. Staff tracks fail when they become a dumping ground for every cross-team bug without a manager partner to reset load.

## How to pick (or push back)

If you want to stay in a product org and grow scope, Staff is the usual path. If the company truly needs a small set of people to bind domains (payments + ledger + fraud), an Architect title can match — **if** it includes veto-with-rationale and time in design reviews that ship. Ask how promotion packets look. If Architect packets are "produced a reference architecture" with no adoption, walk.

Compensation bands should overlap. A title that pays less for more meetings is a trap.

## Working together

Staff in a stream team + architect in an enterprise group: agree who decides at which zoom (C4 levels help). ADRs need an owner. Conflict is normal; unresolved conflict is a dual-track tax.

Read StaffEng archetypes and Reilly's path book for the human skills (writing, facilitation). Then write your own one-pager: scope, not-goals, how success is measured in 12 months. If your manager cannot name the scope, the title will not save you. Tracks are an org's way of paying for leverage. Leverage without a problem is a conference bio.
